# It All Starts with a Link
## Why Atlas begins with bookmarks and pinboards

> **Collection is the beginning of creation.**

Atlas begins with the humble bookmark deliberately. A bookmark is an accessible, lightweight reference to something on the web. It may already bring a title, URL, favicon, preview image, description, and the user's decision that this source matters. A pinboard is already a primitive composition: its members, grouping, order, and annotations express a person's interests and intentions.

This beginning is not a disposable onboarding gimmick. **The bookmark card is the first native example of the Atlas object model**, and the pinboard is its first composition interface. Atlas should make the user's existing bookmarks accessible as reusable objects from the moment they enter the editor, without requiring new uploads, paid compute, or a Cloudflare account.

## Why start here?

People have already done meaningful work by discovering and collecting sources. A collection of relevant bookmarks and preview imagery might account for a meaningful portion of the material for a research project, article, catalog, or personal webpage—perhaps **20% in an illustrative case**, not a measured or promised universal figure. The rest may consist of the user's own notes, captions, analysis, attachments, relationships, and additional sources. Atlas should preserve and compound that earlier work rather than ask them to start with a blank page.

Bookmarks can be organized into reusable groups, selected en masse, added to a project as a united collection, and then sorted, split, rearranged, or individually expanded. Imported bookmark objects should retain stable source identity. Editing their arrangement within a project must not silently modify or delete the original bookmarks.

Images obtained as webpage previews may make early compositions visually rich at little or no infrastructure cost. However, being able to view or reference a preview is not the same as having permission to republish its image. Preserve provenance, links, and rights distinctions; do not assume every referenced image is free to redistribute.

## A student begins with sources, not a blank document

A student researching architecture might collect articles, reference websites, photographs, and a handful of primary sources. Atlas can turn those bookmarks into addressable objects that the student imports into a project, groups into sections, and enriches with notes, quotations, commentary, and relationships.

The pre-template stage can propose sensible organization; the student reviews or edits it before a template engine prepares a coherent article, study guide, bibliography, card, slice, page section, or full webpage. Source-aware notes must preserve citation context and distinguish the student's writing from quotations and referenced content. The student remains the author and final authority over organization and publication.

This principle generalizes beyond students: a designer's pinboard, a researcher's reading list, a small business's catalog, and a collector's archive are different compositions over a shared class of objects.

## From bookmarks to the Transposable Internet

1. **Link:** A connection to a resource.
2. **Bookmark:** A link preserved with identity and available metadata.
3. **Object:** A reference enriched with notes, media, tags, relationships, permissions, and provenance.
4. **Composition:** Objects arranged into meaningful groups, sections, and projects.
5. **Presentation:** A card, slice, expanded section, pop-out, article, or webpage derived from a composition.
6. **Transposition:** Re-presenting the same underlying objects across contexts without erasing their identity or essential relationships.

The user's original materials, approved semantic arrangement, and generated presentation should remain separately recoverable. The editor can permit extensive creative changes; the pre-template engine applies documented conventions and offers a reviewable result, not an irreversible replacement. The user may edit again and regenerate.

Atlas's future unified object interface can extend this model to local files, Cloudflare R2 assets, notes, documents, and remote media by reference, without unnecessary downloading or copying. Agents and template engines should operate on a stable, documented composition model rather than guess intent from arbitrary visual markup.

## Foundational promise

**Start with what you have. Organize what matters. Create something new.**

Atlas begins as a pinboard and bookmark organizer because people already possess the first pieces of their own internet. Its purpose is to turn those pieces into coherent, beautiful, portable, and increasingly agent-understandable work—while leaving the person in control.
